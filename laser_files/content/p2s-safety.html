xTool P2S: Safety

Laser cutters are extremely useful tools, but they introduce hazards that are different from most other machines in the makerspace. For normal users, two of the most important concerns are fire and unsafe materials.

Never Leave the Laser Unattended

You must remain with the P2S whenever it is actively cutting or engraving. Laser processing deliberately heats material to very high temperatures, and many materials used in the machine are flammable.

A small flame can develop into a serious fire quickly. If you need to leave the area, stop or pause the job as appropriate rather than leaving the machine running unattended.

Watch for Fire

Brief flashes can occur during some cutting operations, but a sustained flame is not normal. If material catches fire or the machine appears to be operating unsafely, stop the machine immediately. Use the emergency stop if necessary and notify makerspace staff.

Only Use Approved Materials

Never put an unknown material into the laser cutter. Some plastics and composites can release toxic or corrosive gases when heated by a laser. PVC and vinyl are prohibited. Materials containing unknown coatings, adhesives, or chemical treatments should also be treated as unknown until approved.

If you are unsure what a material is made from, do not use it. Ask makerspace staff before processing anything that is not already on the approved-material list.

Ventilation

The exhaust system must be operating whenever the laser is processing material. If smoke is accumulating inside the machine or escaping into the room, stop the job and notify makerspace staff.

Keep the Machine Clean

Small pieces of wood, paper, acrylic, and other debris can accumulate underneath the cutting area. These scraps can ignite during later jobs. Remove your stock, finished parts, and loose debris when you are finished.

Never Bypass Safety Systems

Never defeat the lid interlock, sensors, enclosure, emergency stop, or any other safety system. Do not operate the machine with panels removed or attempt repairs yourself.

Before Every Job

  1. Confirm that the material is approved.
  2. Make sure the material is lying securely in the machine.
  3. Confirm the correct material and settings are selected.
  4. Make sure the exhaust system is operating.
  5. Check that the machine is free from excessive debris.
  6. Know where the emergency stop and fire extinguisher are located.
  7. Be prepared to remain with the machine for the entire job.
