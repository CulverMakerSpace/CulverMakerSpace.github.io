Design Principles for Laser Cutting

Laser cutters can produce extremely accurate parts, but good results begin with a design that takes the manufacturing process into account. A design that looks correct on the computer will not always produce the intended physical result.

A laser cutter does not produce an infinitely thin cut. The beam removes a small amount of material as it travels along a path. The actual thickness of the material can also differ slightly from its labeled thickness, and those differences matter when parts need to fit together.

Questions to Ask Before Designing

  • Will the project be cut, engraved, scored, or use several operations?
  • How thick is the actual material?
  • Do multiple parts need to fit together?
  • Which dimensions or edges need to be precise?
  • Are any features so small or close together that they may become fragile?
  • Can the parts be arranged to reduce wasted material?

Thinking about these questions before you begin can save material, reduce failed attempts, and produce parts that fit together more reliably.
